About

I work on the reliability of evaluation itself: how we establish that a system generating language is behaving safely, when the instruments we normally use to check it have no coverage.

My current work builds computational resources for C'Lela, a Western Kainji language of north-west Nigeria absent from FLORES, NLLB and the major multilingual corpora, together with a reference-free safety layer that detects clinically dangerous errors in machine-translated health information — dosages, medicine names and negations — in settings where learned quality-estimation models cannot be constructed.

I also work on explainability and evaluation in abuse detection, where recent work examines the limits of explanation-guided debiasing. I teach programming, computer architecture, and systems analysis, and supervise undergraduate research projects through to peer-reviewed publication.

Research

Evaluation past the coverage boundary

Learned evaluators — reward models, COMET, LLM-as-judge — encode the preferences of the populations whose judgments were collected. Outside that distribution they do not fail loudly; they return a plausible number. Any pipeline that reranks, filters or gates on that number will behave as though it has a safety signal while having none. I want to find where that boundary actually lies, and what verifiable, constraint-based signal remains beyond it.

Resources and safety evaluation for C'Lela

A Kainji language of roughly 100,000 speakers in Kebbi State, Nigeria, with no learned quality model and no realistic path to one. Corpus construction, lexical resources, and severity-weighted checks on content preservation — did the dose survive, did the negation survive, is there output content with no source anchor. Conducted with community contacts and a collaborating data scientist.

Explainability in abuse detection

Whether explanation methods can be used to improve models rather than only describe them — and when they cannot. An explanation can be locally faithful and still carry no usable signal about what a model should do.
